Lesson 01 — Driving & Measurement

Concepts: wheel diameter → distance, rotations vs. degrees vs. seconds, why "seconds" is unreliable (battery level changes speed).

Warm-up challenge (5 min)

"Drive from the wall and stop exactly on the far line." Closest robot wins. Let them try with time-based moves — they'll discover the inconsistency.

Teach (10 min)

  • Distance = wheel circumference × rotations. Measure wheel diameter,

    • circumference = π × diameter. For standard SPIKE wheels (~5.6 cm diameter), one rotation ≈ 17.6 cm — have kids measure theirs.

  • In Word Blocks: move [A+B] for [X] rotations. X = target cm ÷ circumference.
  • Port view / sensor values: show motor position readings live.

Hands-on (25 min)

  1. Drive exactly 50 cm. Measure with a ruler. Adjust. Repeat until ±1 cm.
  2. Drive to a specific line on the field mat and stop.
  3. Mini-challenge: "Deliver the package" — push a small object into a taped

    4. target zone from a fixed start. Best of 3 attempts.

Debrief (5 min)

  • Which was more accurate, guessing seconds or calculating rotations? Why?
  • Where does error still come from? (wheel slip, alignment at start)

Success criteria

Can hit a target line within ±2 cm, twice in a row Can explain rotations → distance in their own words
