Curated starting points. The absolute must-haves are the official season materials — everything else is enrichment.

Season facts (verified Aug 2026)

  • Official styling: BIOGLOW™, one word, inside the FIRST® CANOPY™

    • season ("ENGINEER A THRIVING PLANET"). Theme: biodiversity, not bioluminescence. Global release: Aug 4, 2026.

  • 2026–27 is the final FIRST LEGO League season (FIRST–LEGO Group

    • partnership ends after this year).

  • Two editions: Founders Edition = classic program (SPIKE Prime,

    • autonomous 2.5-minute matches — almost certainly yours) vs Future Edition (new CS/AI kits, alliance game). Confirm which your event runs.

  • LEGO Education's SPIKE purchase window closed June 30, 2026 — source kits

    • secondhand if adding more.

Registration & budget notes (2026–27, US)

  • Official new-team bundle: $900 = national registration + SPIKE Prime +

    • Challenge Set. Since you own the kit already, expect à-la-carte registration (last season ~$225 — this season's standalone price not published; check the FIRST Storefront) plus a regional event fee set by your Program Delivery Partner (commonly ~$50–100, varies).

  • Flow: FIRST account → Dashboard "Create a New Team" → FIRST Storefront

    • purchase → contact your Program Delivery Partner to join local events.

Learning SPIKE Prime (kids)

  • PrimeLessons.org — https://primelessons.org/en/Lessons.html — free, the

    • gold standard (by the EV3Lessons/Prime Lessons community). Word Blocks curriculum in 10 units: getting started → beginner movement (straight, gyro turns) → good practices (pseudocode, comments) → sensors → intermediate (line follower, stall detection) → variables & My Blocks → advanced (squaring on lines, proportional/PID line following, gyro-straight, acceleration) → Unit 10 is explicitly FLL competition skills (debugging, reliability). Parallel Python track: https://primelessons.org/en/PyLessons.html

  • FLL Tutorials — https://flltutorials.com — the companion hub:
  • Robot Game: https://flltutorials.com/en/RobotGame.html — 8-part

    • navigation series (finding/aligning on lines, wall following, launch alignment, line following) + decks on passive attachments, gyro sensor positioning, color-sensor shielding, cable management, mission strategy

  • Base robot builds (one-kit, proven): DroidBot 3M, Educator++,

    • Coop Bot — under flltutorials.com/en/robotgame/building/

  • Innovation Project lesson sequence: https://flltutorials.com/en/Project.html
  • flltools — http://flltools.flltutorials.com — free online scorer

    • (use with real BIOGLOW point values), field sketch planner, judging rubrics

  • Coach Corner articles: hours-to-budget, engineering journals, first

    • tournament, scrimmages

  • LEGO Education — SPIKE lesson library & software:

    • https://www.lego.com/en-us/teach/ · https://www.lego.com/en-us/downloads/

Word Blocks vs Python at this age: start everyone on Word Blocks — loops, conditionals, variables, and My Blocks all transfer if a kid switches later. Python suits a motivated 6th grader mid-season; the PrimeLessons Python track mirrors the same lessons. Timeline: driving/turning within 2–4 meetings; real missions by weeks 4–8; proportional line following is a late-season refinement, not a prerequisite.

Coaching craft

  • Read the Team Meeting Guide cover to cover once — it encodes FIRST's

    • pedagogy (kid-led, discovery-based). Treat it as a floor, not a ceiling; competitive teams usually run more meetings than its ~12 sessions.

  • Budget rule of thumb: competitive teams put in 2–6+ hours/week (you have 4 —

    • healthy for a small mixed team).

  • Your regional Program Delivery Partner runs coach workshops/webinars early

    • in the season — one evening, huge payoff. A pre-qualifier scrimmage is the single best tournament prep (flltutorials.com Coach Corner has an article on finding/running one).

  • Veteran coach threads: official FIRST community forums + r/FLL on Reddit —

    • search "rookie coach."

For the Innovation Project (biodiversity)

  • iNaturalist (inaturalist.org) + the Seek app — identify species; perfect

    • for a schoolyard bioblitz

  • National Geographic & WWF biodiversity explainers — accessible background
  • Your county conservation district / extension office — local data and experts
  • NOAA and USFWS educational resources — oceans, endangered species recovery
  • Global Innovation Award winner archives (firstinspires.org/robotics/fll/

    • global-innovation) — study what winning projects look like: quantified problems, working prototypes, real implementation steps

  • If the team pivots toward a living-light angle (see

    • problem-brainstorm): Nobel Prize GFP classroom materials; Light Bio's bioluminescent Firefly Petunia (first USDA-cleared glowing plant, 2025) as a sustainable-lighting discussion starter; dinoflagellate-bay conservation stories (Mosquito Bay, Vieques)
