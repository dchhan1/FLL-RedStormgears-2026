Seed ideas in the biodiversity space (verify against the official project brief)

Local & actionable (easy to research, easy to find experts):

  • Pollinator decline: bees, butterflies, monarchs — what local action actually works?
  • Habitat loss near us: a woods, wetland, or field being developed — what's lost, what would offset it?
  • Invasive species: which one is damaging a local ecosystem, and how do we detect/limit it?
  • Stream or pond health: what's living in our creek, and what does it say about water quality?
  • Schoolyard/backyard biodiversity: run a mini bioblitz, find the gap, design a fix

Bigger-stage problems:

  • Species monitoring: how do scientists count animals? (camera traps, acoustic surveys, eDNA — a tech-solution angle)
  • Urban wildlife coexistence: corridors, green roofs, bird-window strikes, road crossings
  • Light pollution disrupting insects, fireflies, migrating birds, sea turtles (bridges to the "glow" in BIOGLOW)
  • Soil & fungi: the underground network that feeds forests
  • Ocean ecosystems: overfishing, bycatch, reef restoration
  • One endangered local species: its recovery plan and what's blocking it

If the official materials lean into living light (unconfirmed): GFP in medicine, glowing plants as lighting, deep-sea research tools, bioluminescent pollution sensors, dinoflagellate bays, firefly decline.
