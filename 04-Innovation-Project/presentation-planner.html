Presentation Planner

Confirm this season's exact format and time limit in the official documents — typically ~5 minutes to present, then judge Q&A. Verify before scripting.

Story spine (a structure that works)

  1. Hook (15s) — a question, image, or demo that grabs attention
  2. The problem (45s) — who it affects, why it matters, with evidence
  3. Our research (45s) — what we learned, who we talked to
  4. Our solution (90s) — how it works, what makes it innovative
  5. Feedback & iteration (45s) — what changed because of expert/user feedback
  6. Sharing & close (30s) — whom we shared it with; call to action

Rules of a strong presentation

  • Setup time usually counts against your minutes — keep props minimal and

    • kid-handled, and rehearse the setup itself.

  • Every kid speaks. Judges notice and it's part of the rubric.
  • Speak from memory or kid-made cue cards — never an adult-written script read aloud.
  • Show the prototype. A prop beats a paragraph.
  • Winning arc: hook (skit/demo) → problem with data → solution/prototype →

    • how experts shaped it → how you shared it.

  • Skits, songs, and demos are all allowed — play to the kids' personalities.

Judge Q&A prep (practice these every rehearsal)

  • Why did you choose this problem? · Why this solution over the others you found?
  • How does it actually work? · What would you do next with more time/money?
  • What was the hardest part? · Who on the team did what?
  • How did you use your experts' feedback?
  • Random wildcard questions — practice staying calm and honest ("we don't know,

    • but we'd find out by ___" is a great answer)
