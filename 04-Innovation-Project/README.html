Innovation Project — BIOGLOW

The Innovation Project asks the team to research a real-world problem related to the season theme and present an innovative solution. Official BIOGLOW materials frame the theme as biodiversity — the vibrancy of the world's ecosystems, and how the balance between nature and humans keeps our world healthy. Read this season's Challenge Guide closely once registered — if the missions or project brief give a more specific "glow"-related angle (some community coverage suspects living light), fold that in; until then, biodiversity is the verified framing.

The project arc

  1. Learn the theme → brainstorm problems (many!)
  2. Choose one problem and define it precisely
  3. Research it — including talking to real experts
  4. Design an innovative solution
  5. Prototype & get feedback — then iterate once
  6. Share it beyond the team
  7. Present the whole story to judges (~5 min + Q&A, per season rules)

Why experts matter

Teams that interview even one real expert consistently do better in Project judging — it makes the work real and gives kids stories to tell. For a biodiversity theme, good candidates: ecologists and wildlife biologists, university biology / environmental-science departments, park naturalists, conservation nonprofits and land trusts, zoo/aquarium educators, county environmental or extension offices.

Coach guardrails

  • Kids choose the problem (they'll defend it for months — it must be theirs).
  • Coach may drive the car to the interview; kids ask the questions.
  • "Innovative" doesn't mean never-done-before; it means new to them or a

    • genuinely creative twist. Aim for a solution concept they can explain simply and defend with research.

  • Expert interviews are the #1 project bottleneck — start outreach in week 3,

    • not week 10.
