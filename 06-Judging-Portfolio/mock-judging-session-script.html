Mock Judging Session Script (for the adult playing judge)

Run this twice, in weeks 12 and 13, with a non-coach adult if possible (another parent works great — unfamiliar faces add realistic pressure). Give this script to the "judge"; the team should NOT read it beforehand.

Note: event formats vary by region — some run three separate sessions as below; others combine everything into one ~30-minute judging block (project presentation, robot Q&A, and a teamwork activity back-to-back). Check your event's schedule and practice whichever shape you'll face; the content below covers both.

Setup

Room with table and chairs, ~10 minutes per session, team brings its portfolio. Judge stays friendly but neutral — no coaching during the session.

Session 1 — Innovation Project (~10 min)

  1. "Welcome! Tell us about your project." (team presents, ~5 min — time it)
  2. Q&A (~5 min), pick 3–4:
  3. Why this problem?
  4. How do you know your research is accurate?
  5. What makes your solution innovative?
  6. How did you use your expert's feedback?
  7. What would you improve with another month?
  8. One wildcard: "If your solution existed tomorrow, who would use it first?"

Session 2 — Robot Design (~10 min)

  1. "Show us your robot and tell us how you designed it." (~5 min)
  2. Q&A, pick 3–4:
  3. Walk me through what this program does. (point at the longest one)
  4. Why did you choose this drive base / these wheels?
  5. Show me an attachment and why you built it that way.
  6. What broke most often? How did you fix it?
  7. Which mission is hardest, and why?
  8. Ask a quiet member one question directly: "What part did you work on?"

Session 3 — Core Values (~10 min)

  1. Surprise hands-on challenge (5 min): give the team an untried task with

    2. simple materials — e.g., paper bridge spanning two books that holds a marker, or "invent a game using these 3 objects." Watch silently:

  2. Does everyone participate? Do they listen? Do they build on each other's ideas?
  3. Q&A (5 min):
  4. What's a time this season your team disagreed? What happened?
  5. Which Core Value was hardest for your team?
  6. What will you remember from this season in five years?

After all three: judge's feedback form

AreaWhat impressed meOne thing to work on
Innovation Project
Robot Design
Core Values

Deliver feedback kindly and only after all sessions end. Coach writes none of it down for the kids — the team records what they took from it.
